# Hook Songs in 50-word bites

**The idea.** One- to two-minute house, afrobeats, liquid or jungle tracks where the catchy hook *is* the Greek word. Each song has 3–5 words, mostly ones you already learned. It is repetitive on purpose, silly on purpose. A fun bonus track every five lessons, and shareable clips that bring new learners.

**The trick.** These genres already use chopped vocal samples instead of singers. So our checked TTS clips become the vocal instrument: chopped, pitched and repeated on the beat. The pronunciation is always correct, which matters most. A catchy wrong word would be worse than no song.

**Song shape.** The MC says the English, then the Greek is heard clean. The build-up riser plays the syllable pieces (στώ… ριστώ…). Then one bar of silence while you say it, and the drop lands on the word. A call-and-response break follows, then the outro asks without answering. It is a Pimsleur lesson disguised as a track.

**Making the beat.** The cheapest and cleanest option is beats synthesized by our own code: free, exact timing, no copyright worries. Later we can add AI instrumentals: ACE-Step (open, runs locally or on rented GPUs) or Google Lyria 3 (about $0.08 per song). We test them against each other by ear.

**Making the voice.** Step 1: spoken TTS chops, like house vocal samples. Step 2: auto-tuned chops that follow the melody. Step 3, research only: AI actually singing Greek. A speech recognizer checks every word before you hear it, because AI singers mispronounce smaller languages.

**Cost and legal.** Code-made beats cost $0, and AI beds about $0.03–0.15 per song. We never use copyrighted samples (not even the famous Amen break), non-commercial models like MusicGen, or artist names in prompts. We check each model's license file before using it.

**Best path.** Week 1: four 30-second sketches so you can judge by ear. Then a small renderer for deep house songs from Module 1. Then the other genres, auto-tune and ratings in BackOffice. Then AI beds if they sound better. AI singing comes last, only if it passes the pronunciation check.

**What I need from you.** Listen to the sketches and rate them honestly. Decide: bonus tracks inside the lesson app or a separate playlist? Is the MC the same voice as the lesson narrator, or a funny separate character? Maybe later, some human-made intro music for brand identity.
